
# Project Context for AI Assistance

## Project Overview

- **Name:** buy-and-sell-market
- **Purpose:** A managed marketplace where sellers send items to the platform's warehouse. The platform verifies, stores, and fulfills inventory so sellers have limited direct interaction with buyers.
- **Domain:** Consumer-to-consumer marketplace with centralized warehousing and fulfillment.
- **Initial scale target:** 10,000 daily active users and 50,000 listed items. Daily active users do not define peak concurrent traffic; peak load and growth targets must be established before launch.
- **Project stage:** Requirements and production architecture are proposals. No implementation, cloud provider, service-level objective, or external provider has been confirmed.
- **Operating assumption:** The platform handles physical custody, order orchestration, and customer support; a dedicated payment processor executes and records payment transactions, refunds, and seller payouts. Whether legal ownership transfers to the platform at any point, and the seller payout/return policies, require explicit business decisions.

## Technical Stack

- **Frontend:** Proposed React, Vite, and TypeScript for buyer, seller, admin, and warehouse operations interfaces.
- **Backend:** Proposed Node.js, TypeScript, and Express as a modular monolith exposing a versioned REST API. Publish an OpenAPI contract so frontend clients and API tests can verify request/response compatibility. Keep catalog, identity, inventory, order, payment-provider integration, fulfillment, and administration as clear modules; the payment module orchestrates provider requests and reconciliation but does not process card data or move funds itself. Split services only when measured scale or team ownership warrants it.
- **Database:** Proposed managed PostgreSQL for production and a separate resettable PostgreSQL test database. Core records include users and roles, private account profiles, payment-provider customer/payment-method tokens, listings, inventory units and movements, warehouse locations, orders, shipments, returns, and audit events. Provide deterministic seed data for each role and scripts to reset and populate test data in non-production environments. Do not store raw card data. Use indexes and transaction constraints to protect inventory and order consistency.
- **Infrastructure:** Proposed cloud deployment with separate development, staging, and production environments. Store item images in object storage and serve them through a CDN. Use a managed queue for retryable background work such as notifications and shipping-provider updates. Add caching only for measured hot paths; do not start with microservices or a dedicated search cluster without evidence they are needed.
- **External services:** A dedicated payment processor handles buyer charges, refunds, seller payouts, and payment-method storage/tokenization. Provider selection is undecided. Integrate through a provider adapter and use sandbox accounts in non-production environments. Confirm support for seller payouts in launch regions; card payouts may not be available everywhere. The marketplace stores provider references and transaction status, never raw card details. Shipping-label/tracking and email providers are also undecided.
- **Testing tools:** Proposed Vitest for unit tests, Playwright with TypeScript for browser end-to-end journeys, and a load-testing tool such as k6 for agreed traffic targets. Playwright should exercise the real backend through its public API and browser flows. Provide deterministic setup/cleanup and test accounts without authentication bypasses; use payment-provider sandboxes or a test adapter for payment scenarios. Add API contract tests against the OpenAPI definition. These tools and practices are proposals, not configured in the repository.

## Key Features & Modules

1. **Accounts and access:** Buyer and Seller registration requires name, email, address, phone number, and a payment method for marketplace transactions. Sellers also complete processor onboarding and provide an eligible payout destination. Admin and Warehouse Operator accounts are provisioned separately and protected with stronger access controls.
2. **Seller catalog:** Sellers list a broad range of household and personal belongings, such as phones, watches, laptops, desks, monitors, and TVs. Each listing includes a title, description, category, price, condition (`new`, `used`, or `old`), and at least one photo. Sellers send associated items to the warehouse. A listing is not purchasable until required inventory has been received and checked in.
3. **Warehouse operations:** Receive inbound seller shipments, identify and count items, record condition and discrepancies, assign storage locations, and track every inventory movement. Operators pick, pack, and dispatch paid orders and process returns.
4. **Buyer catalog and checkout:** Buyers search and filter available items, view accurate stock/status, place orders, and receive order and shipment updates.
5. **Orders and payment integration:** Record order state and provider references, prevent overselling, request charges/refunds/payouts from the dedicated processor, and reconcile its confirmed statuses and events. Marketplace fees and seller payout rules still require definition; the processor executes the resulting money movements.
6. **Fulfillment and returns:** Create shipment records and tracking updates; support return authorization, warehouse receipt, inspection, restocking or write-off, and request eligible refunds from the payment processor.
7. **Administration and reporting:** Admins manage users, moderate listings, monitor orders and inventory exceptions, and review audit history. Warehouse staff should use a narrower operational role rather than general Admin access.

## Current Testing Approach

- **Manual testing:** No established test process or test environments yet. Plan role-based exploratory testing for the catalog, warehouse receiving, checkout, dispatch, returns, and admin workflows.
- **Automation:** Not yet configured. Proposed coverage: unit tests for domain rules; API and database integration tests for permissions, inventory, order transitions, and retries; a small Playwright suite for critical end-to-end journeys.
- **Performance:** No measured baseline or SLO exists. Test with 50,000 catalog items and a representative data set. Define peak concurrent users, request mix, and growth headroom from expected usage, then load-test browse/search, item detail, checkout, and warehouse operations against those targets.
- **Reliability:** Test database backup/restore, queue retry and dead-letter handling, payment-processor timeouts, duplicate/out-of-order payment events and webhooks, idempotent charge/refund/payout requests, and recovery from partial fulfillment failures.
- **Security:** No implementation exists yet. Test authentication, least-privilege role access, object-level authorization, input validation, rate limiting on sensitive endpoints, secure session handling, and auditability of administrative and inventory changes. Include privacy and access-control tests for names, contact details, addresses, and payment tokens. Keep secrets out of source control and use synthetic data outside production.

## Pain Points & Known Issues

- **Recurring bugs:** None known; the application has not been implemented.
- **Performance bottlenecks:** None measured. Potential risks to validate include catalog query performance, image delivery, checkout contention, and warehouse inventory reconciliation.
- **Testing gaps:** Peak concurrency, service objectives, personal-data retention rules, processor support in launch regions, return/refund policy, marketplace fee and seller payout rules, warehouse exception handling, and provider contracts need definition before production readiness can be assessed.
- **Operational risks:** Inventory counts can diverge from physical stock; duplicate or delayed provider events can corrupt state if operations are not idempotent; warehouse outages can delay dispatch. Define reconciliation, exception queues, and operational ownership.

## Business Rules & Constraints

- **Critical user journeys:** Buyer or Seller registers with the required profile details and adds a payment method; Seller completes payout onboarding, creates a listing, and ships items to the warehouse; warehouse receives and inspects items and makes accepted stock available; Buyer orders available stock and pays; warehouse picks, packs, and dispatches the order; Buyer tracks delivery or requests a return; warehouse inspects returns and the platform updates stock/refund state.
- **Inventory:** Track each physical unit or controlled quantity by listing/SKU, condition (`new`, `used`, or `old`), and warehouse location. Only received, inspected, sellable stock is available for checkout. Inventory reservation and order placement must be atomic enough to prevent overselling. Every adjustment must have a reason and audit record.
- **Listing and custody:** Support a broad range of household and personal items, subject to a prohibited-item and safety policy that must be defined before launch. Each listing must include at least one photo and a condition selected from `new`, `used`, or `old`; reject listings with no photo or an unsupported condition. Sellers may edit listing details subject to inventory and order state. Warehouse receipt records expected versus actual quantity and condition. Discrepancies require resolution before affected stock is sellable; record both seller-declared and warehouse-inspected condition where they differ. Custody, ownership, loss/damage liability, and disposal rules require legal/business definition.
- **Account requirements:** Buyer and Seller accounts require name, unique email, address, phone number, and a payment method handled by the chosen payment processor. Sellers must also complete processor onboarding and provide a supported payout destination. Define required-field validation, consent, and personal-data retention periods before launch.
- **Orders and money:** The dedicated payment processor is responsible for executing buyer charges, refunds, and seller payouts, and for securely collecting/storing payment methods. The marketplace creates payment/refund/payout requests, applies approved order and refund rules, and reconciles provider-confirmed status using signed events and provider references. Seller proceeds go to a processor-supported payout destination; if card payouts are unavailable in a launch region, an approved alternative such as bank payout is required. Define cancellation, returns, refund eligibility, marketplace fees, and seller payout timing before launch. The marketplace must not store card numbers or represent a requested transaction as completed until the processor confirms it.
- **Permissions:** Buyers access their own orders; Sellers access their own listings, inbound shipments, and settlement information; Warehouse Operators access assigned warehouse workflows; Admin actions are restricted and audited. Enforce authorization on the server for every protected resource.
- **Compliance requirements:** Applicable privacy, consumer protection, tax, and payment requirements depend on launch countries and are not yet determined. Complete a jurisdictional and payment-provider review before production launch, including seller onboarding requirements and the processor's responsibility boundaries. Keep card entry and processing within the provider's hosted/tokenized flow to limit exposure, and confirm applicable payment-security obligations with the provider and compliance advisers.
- **Performance and availability objectives:** None approved. Establish measurable SLOs for availability, latency, data recovery, and peak load before launch; daily active user count alone is insufficient to size the system.
- **Security policies:** Use secure password hashing, TLS in transit, encryption at rest where supported, least-privilege access, managed secrets, audit logs, and a documented backup/restore process. Treat account details and contact/address information as sensitive personal data. Use processor-hosted/tokenized card collection and store only provider tokens/references. Define consent, retention, deletion, access logging, breach response, and restricted staff access before launch.

## Sample Data & User Roles

- **Buyer:** Register with name, email, address, phone number, and processor-managed payment method; browse available items, place and pay for orders, view their own order/shipment status, and request returns.
- **Seller:** Register with name, email, address, and phone number; complete processor-managed payment and payout setup; create listings, prepare inbound shipments, view receipt discrepancies, and see sales and settlement status for their own items.
- **Warehouse Operator (proposed additional role):** Receive, inspect, locate, pick, pack, dispatch, and process returned inventory. Access is limited to assigned operational tasks and locations.
- **Admin:** Manage accounts and listings, resolve exceptions, configure marketplace operations, and review audit records. Admin access is not a substitute for routine warehouse permissions.
- **Test accounts:** Use separate synthetic accounts for every role and include unauthorized cross-account attempts in tests. Cover missing or invalid profile fields, duplicate email, payment authorization failure, payout onboarding failure, provider outage, listing without a photo, invalid condition, and warehouse condition discrepancy scenarios using sandboxes or test fixtures. Never use real personal data, payment cards, or production credentials in test environments.
- **Data sets:** Generate at least 50,000 synthetic listings across household and personal-item categories, with at least one photo reference and a condition (`new`, `used`, or `old`) per listing, plus inventory units, warehouse locations, orders, shipments, returns, and lifecycle edge cases. Include unavailable, damaged, reserved, sold, and returned stock.
- **Environments:** Proposed development, staging, and production environments. Staging should use synthetic data and payment/shipping sandbox integrations; production access and data must be restricted.
